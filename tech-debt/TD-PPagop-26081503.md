Found reviewing TD-PPagop-26081406 (PR #389). That item's enumeration came
from a `grep -n -- --argjson` over `agent-cycle.sh`, `lib/` and `scripts/`,
and it converted every site that grep named. What the grep could not show is
that its own list was not the whole residue: requirement 4g's rule is about
what rides in a single argv element, and these sites still carry a fleet-state
aggregate there.

None is known to be near `MAX_ARG_STRLEN` (131072 bytes) today. They are
listed loudest-failure-first, on the same terms TD-PPagop-26081401 and
TD-PPagop-26081406 used.

- `scripts/gather-source-state.sh:182-186` — `--argjson issues`,
  `--argjson workflows` and `--argjson open_prs` in the final state build:
  one repo's whole open-issue list, workflow list and open-PR list. Three
  separate unbounded values in one `execve`, and the largest single exposure
  on this list.
- `scripts/gather-findings.sh:138` — `--argjson dep` and `--argjson cs`, a
  repo's whole Dependabot and code-scanning alert lists. A repo with a large
  backlog of open alerts is exactly the repo whose `findings` band matters
  most, and past the cap it comes back empty.
- `scripts/gather-register-hygiene.sh:289,302,303` — `--argjson a`/`--argjson
  b` in the problems merge, and `--argjson problems` plus `--arg body` in the
  final build. This is the direct analogue of
  `scripts/gather-human-visibility-hygiene.sh`'s own problems-and-body build,
  which TD-PPagop-26081406 converted; the two scripts have the same shape and
  the same growth, and only one of them was on that item's list. Convert both
  values together — the body is rendered from the same set as the problems
  array and is the larger of the two, so moving only the array leaves the
  threshold where it was.
- `scripts/publish-dashboard.sh:1698` — `--argjson prs` and `--argjson claims`
  in the `github_json` build: the whole cross-repo PR index and the claims
  cache, both of which grow with the fleet. Requirement 4g says in terms that
  "The rule is the Publisher's too", and several neighbouring Publisher sites
  were converted for exactly this reason; these two were not.

The fix is the same mechanical conversion the two predecessor items
established: concatenate the documents (`docs="$(printf '%s\n' "$a" "$b")"`),
deliver with a here-string, bind positionally with `input as $name` in the
printed order, and leave only configuration-bounded values in argv. Convert
each site together with a test that pins its fail-safe direction past the cap,
as requirement 4g's own acceptance discipline requires —
`test/review-feedback.test.sh` and `test/gather-human-visibility-hygiene.test.sh`
are the nearest references, and both show the extracted-block technique for a
build that cannot be driven end-to-end. Where a converted site currently fails
loudly, keep it loud.

Requirement 4g in `docs/IMPLEMENTATION-PIPELINE-SPEC.md` names this item,
alongside TD-PPagop-26081501 and TD-PPagop-26081502, as the residue
outstanding after TD-PPagop-26081406; resolving all three is what would let
that requirement claim the sweep is tree-wide.
