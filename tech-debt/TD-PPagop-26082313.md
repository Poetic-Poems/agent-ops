Requirement 17f (`refinement_traceability_fault`, `agent-cycle.sh`,
agent-ops#626, PR #702) added a third way for the claim loop to drop a
candidate without attempting its claim: a `claim-skipped` event with
`cause: "untraceable"`. Unlike the two that were already there, it feeds no
counter — deliberately, because `claim_skips` is documented at its own
declaration as meaning *pre-claimed* alone, so that the dashboard's
`↻ raced` badge keeps meaning only healthy contention.

The stand-down classifier immediately below the loop was not taught about
it, and it decides between its three causes from exactly those counters. So
a cycle in which *every* ranked candidate is untraceable reaches the
classifier with `claim_attempts == 0`, `claim_unreachable == 0` and
`claim_skips == 0`, falls through both guarded branches, and lands in the
`else`:

- it logs `stand-down` with `cause: "raced"` and the reason text `every
  candidate is already claimed elsewhere` — of a cycle in which nothing was
  claimed anywhere, by anyone;
- the same event carries `race_losses: 0`, so the record contradicts itself
  on its own face: a raced stand-down that lost no races;
- `raced` is the one cause that *chains* (requirement 39). The cycle
  therefore buys another Co-Ordinator engagement, up to `max_chained_cycles`
  of them, on the strength of a contention reading that is not true.

## Why it matters

The chaining is the expensive half. A fresh Co-Ordinator engagement may well
compose a traceable work order the second time — chaining is not obviously
the *wrong* behaviour here — but the cycle is buying it for a stated reason
that is false, and where the fault is deterministic (a spec whose paste
drifts the same way every cycle, per TD-PPagop-26082307) it buys the
same empty result every time. The misreporting is the durable half: the
`raced` cause and the `↻ raced` badge are the fleet's only cheap signal for
"peers are contending", and requirement 17f can now raise it without a peer
being involved at all. The `pre-claimed` cause exists precisely because a
selection defect was judged worth its own name rather than being folded into
`raced`; an untraceable work order is the same kind of defect and has the
same claim.

## Where

- `agent-cycle.sh` — the claim loop's `cause: "untraceable"` skip, the
  `claim_skips` declaration and its comment, and the
  `claim_attempts`/`claim_unreachable`/`claim_skips` classifier that follows
  the loop (`standdown_reason`/`standdown_cause`, and the `raced`-only
  `chain_eligible` test).
- `docs/IMPLEMENTATION-PIPELINE-SPEC.md` — requirement 17f, and whichever
  requirement enumerates the stand-down causes.

## Suggested fix

Give the skip its own counter and its own cause — `untraceable` — rather
than folding it into `claim_skips`, which would corrupt the `pre-claimed`
signal the same way `raced` is being corrupted now. Then decide the chaining
question explicitly rather than by fall-through: a chained cycle re-runs the
Co-Ordinator from a fresh gather and so is a plausible way out, which argues
for chaining, but it should be chosen on that reasoning and recorded in the
spec, not inherited from a misclassification. Spec requirement 17f and the
stand-down cause list need the new name either way.

Noticed by the Reviewer stage while reviewing PR #702
(https://github.com/Poetic-Poems/agent-ops/pull/702), which introduces
requirement 17f; out of scope for that pull request, whose own remit is the
cross-item join fault itself.
