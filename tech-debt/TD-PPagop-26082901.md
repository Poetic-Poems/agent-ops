Noticed while resolving escalations #905, #906, #910 and #911 by hand on
2026-08-29. Each of the three cycles that raised #905, #906 and #910 said "that
race is being filed as a tech-debt record this cycle", and none of the three
filings landed — the shape `TD-PPagop-26082804` records; `td/TD-PPagop-26082840`
to `-42` are reservations with no file behind them.

## What

`ENABLER_ELIGIBLE_JQ` (`lib/cycle-state.sh`) grants the `issue-closed` reason
only when the item's latest `escalated` event is later than its latest block B:

```
$escalation.ts > $b.ts
```

That compares against the escalation's *raise* time. When a fresh block lands
after the escalation was raised — a Co-Ordinator `needs-refinement` re-flag, in
every observed case — B moves past the escalation, and the human's close,
whenever it comes, can no longer satisfy the rule. The item falls back to
`threshold`, where requirement 36b's thrash guard
(`refinement_second_pass_refused`, whose only exemption is this very reason)
refuses a second refinement without a human touch, so the only exit is a fresh
escalation asking the human to say again what they already said.

Observed on three items, all re-blocked by one Co-Ordinator run on 2026-08-27
(labels applied 23:17:57–23:18:24Z; on #810 the block's own `detail` read
"Owner decision already settled … actionable now"):

| item | escalation raised | closed | re-block | second escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #706 | #849, 08-27T19:35:02Z | 08-27T20:30:58Z | 08-27T23:18:06Z | #905 |
| #779 | #784, 08-24T21:35:11Z | 08-25T04:27:31Z | 08-27T23:18:16Z | #906 |
| #810 | #813, 08-26T00:57:44Z | 08-26T06:37:20Z | 08-27T23:18:27Z | #910 |

#706 is the sharpest case: the close came 2 h 47 m *before* the re-block, no
engagement examined the item in that window, and the re-block then stranded
the close. For #779 and #810 the closes were days earlier and had already been
acted on (`adjudicate-first` unblocked both on 08-27, at 19:36Z and 19:02Z);
their re-flags asked for the very decisions those closed escalations record,
and the rule offered no path back to them.

## Why it matters

Requirement 36a promises the human that closing the escalation issue is what
restarts the work. Under this rule the promise holds only if nothing re-blocks
the item between the raise and the next Enabler pass. A re-flag is cheap and
frequent (#683, `TD-PPagop-26082816`), an Enabler pass runs at most once per
cycle, and the loser is the human, who is asked the same question twice
(#849 → #905) — the exact cost #627 exists to remove. Every such round trip also
spends an Enabler engagement at Opus prices to establish that nothing changed.

## Suggested fix

Key the exemption on the human's act, not on the raise. Two workable shapes;
the first is the smaller:

1. **Compare against the block the escalation was raised for.** `escalated`
   events already carry `blocked_ts` (`lib/enabler.sh`). Grant `issue-closed`
   when the latest escalation is closed and no examination has followed it,
   and *either* `$escalation.ts > $b.ts` (today's rule) *or* the current
   block is a `needs-refinement` re-flag whose `refined_before` predates the
   escalation — the escalation was raised against the same specification the
   re-flag disputes, so the human's close answers the re-flag too. The
   exemption grants an *examination*, not an unblock: the engagement still
   verifies the closed issue's claim against reality (`prompts/enabler.md`),
   so nothing is released on the strength of a stale close.
2. **Read the close time.** Extend the source-state digest (or the Enabler's
   live read) with `closedAt` for issues carrying `enabler_escalation_label`,
   and grant `issue-closed` when `closedAt > $b.ts`. More faithful, but the
   digest is deliberately open-numbers-only, and this adds a field every
   consumer must tolerate.

Either way, add the #706 shape to `test/enabler-eligibility.test.sh`:
escalation raised, closed, re-blocked, not examined → eligible with reason
`issue-closed`.
